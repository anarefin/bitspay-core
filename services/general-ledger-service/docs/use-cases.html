general-ledger-service

The Operator's official accounting books: it turns every committed ledger transfer into double-entry journals in the chart of accounts, closes days, months and fiscal years, and publishes trial balance, balance sheet, P&L and a daily ERP file. It never moves money.

1. What this service does, in one minute

The analogy: the company accountant's books

Picture a busy shop. The cash register rings up every sale the moment it happens and keeps the till exact to the poisha. That is ledger-service (backed by TigerBeetle, a high-speed accounting database): it actually moves e-money between wallets, and it is the only place in Bits Pay where money moves.

Every evening the shop's accountant picks up the register tape, sorts each line into the company's official books ("this 5 Tk was P2P fee income", "this 0.75 Tk is VAT we owe the government"), checks that the books balance, closes the day, and at month end and year end closes the month and the year. The accountant also writes entries that never went through the till, such as a bank charge or the opening share capital. The accountant never opens the till. That accountant is general-ledger-service (the "GL").

What it owns

  • The chart of accounts (CoA): the single platform owner, seed v0 with 56 provisional accounts.
  • Posting rules: versioned, effective-dated rule sets (v1 = 51 rules) that map each ledger leg to a GL account.
  • The ledger account registry (ledger account id to bucket such as WALLET:C, SYS:FEE_INCOME, TRUST:01).
  • The sub-ledger summary (stage 1) and one LEDGER_DAY journal per business day and revision (stage 2).
  • Manual journals, accruals, reversals, opening balances, the trust-interest auto-journal (flag, default off).
  • GL days, months, fiscal years and their close; daily balances; control totals for reconciliation.
  • Reports (trial balance, balance sheet, P&L, account statement) and the daily ERP file GENERIC_CSV_V1.
  • Exceptions, staff notifications, maker-checker approvals and audit for its own entities.

What it does not own (SPEC §1.3)

  • Moving money, ledger commands, editing TigerBeetle data: never.
  • Bank statement reconciliation, e-money vs trust variance, ledger to transaction matching: finance-ops (reconciliation). The GL is checked by reconciliation; it never checks itself.
  • Per-wallet statements and transaction history: finance-ops reporting. The GL holds bucket-level data only.
  • Hierarchical commission accrual (R2), real-time ERP push, multi-currency, fixed assets, payroll, AP/AR, back-office UI (Swagger only).
This service must NEVER (SPEC §1.4)
  1. Move money, call a ledger command (POST /v1/ledger/commands/*), or write another service's schema.
  2. Multiply, divide or round money. It only adds amounts the ledger posted (and amounts a maker typed). Money is long poisha (1 Tk = 100 poisha).
  3. Count a ledger leg twice or drop one. A leg is booked exactly once (DB-stored offsets); an unmapped leg goes to 1999 with a CRITICAL exception; an unreadable record raises INVALID_LEDGER_EVENT.
  4. Guess a wallet type. An unknown account is looked up in the ledger, else deferred, else booked to 1999 and re-keyed later.
  5. Hard-code a GL account for a ledger leg in Java. The active rule set decides (exceptions: 1999 for unmapped, 3201 for year-end close).
  6. Post a manual, accrual, reversal, opening or interest line to a ledger-controlled account.
  7. Edit or delete a POSTED journal or line, or change anything dated in a CLOSED period.
  8. Certify its own day. Certification comes only from reconciliation's GL tie-out.
  9. Put personal data (MSISDN, names, NID) in tables, events, logs or audit.
  10. Wait for another service at start-up. Readiness covers Postgres and Kafka only.

2. Where it sits

The GL sits in the back office ("ledger & data" layer). It is a pure consumer of the money path: it reads the ledger's change stream and a few ledger read APIs, a handful of other topics, and serves Finance staff and finance-ops. Solid lines are synchronous HTTP calls; dashed lines are asynchronous Kafka events (SPEC §3.1).

  1. ledger-service → GL: ledger.changes v1 (LEDGER_TRANSFER_POSTED), every committed business leg. The core input.
  2. GL → ledger-service (reads only): GET /v1/ledger/accounts/{id}/balance (account type of an unknown wallet), POST /v1/ledger/accounts/balances:batchGet and GET /v1/ledger/system-balance-sheet (informational panel on a GL day).
  3. wallet-service → GL: wallet.events (WALLET_UPSERTED) fills the ledger account registry (wallet id → wallet type).
  4. treasury-service → GL: treasury.events (TRUST_INTEREST_RECORDED, MANUAL_JOURNAL_POSTED, EMONEY_REDEEMED).
  5. finance-ops-service → GL: recon.events (RECON_RUN_COMPLETED type GL_TIEOUT) certifies GL days.
  6. finance-ops-service → GL: GET /v1/gl/days/{businessDate}/control-totals, GET /v1/gl/days/{businessDate}, GET /v1/gl/accounts, GET /v1/gl/accounts/{code} (the only SERVICE-callable reads).
  7. admin-service → GL: refdata.events (cut-off, grace, time zone, fiscal-year start, calendar).
  8. GL → admin-service: notification.requests, approval.events, audit.events (admin-owned inbound topics).
  9. Finance staff → GL: /v1/gl/* staff APIs and /v1/approvals (roles FIN_MAKER, FIN_CHECKER, AUDITOR).
  10. GL → readers: gl.events (day closed, day certified, period status, journal posted, account upserted, exception raised).
  11. GL → Operator ERP: daily journal file GENERIC_CSV_V1 (downloaded, not pushed).

3. Key concepts and glossary

Double-entry in five minutes

Double-entry bookkeeping means every event is recorded at least twice: once as a debit (Dr, left side) on one account and once as a credit (Cr, right side) on another, for the same amount. Because every entry has equal debits and credits, the total of all debits in the books always equals the total of all credits. If it ever doesn't, something is broken. That is what a trial balance checks.

"Debit" and "credit" do not mean "good" or "bad". Whether a debit increases or decreases an account depends on the account's class and its normal balance side:

4.2 Posting rules

Posting rules are data, not code (ADR-GL-04). One RuleEngine is shared by ingestion-time derivation, restatement, :simulate and the open-day report overlay. A rule maps one side of one leg to one GL account, so every leg yields exactly one debit line and one credit line of the same amount (ADR-GL-05).

How a rule wins (BR-GL-20). For each side of a leg, build the fact {tbAccountType, side, transferCode, commandType, legIndex, reasonCode, counterpartyAccountType}. Candidates are rules of the applicable set whose tbAccountType equals and whose every specified key equals. Winner = most specified keys; tie → lowest priority; still tied → the set is invalid. No candidate → 1999 + CRITICAL UNMAPPED_POSTING. 4.3 Ledger ingestion: how a ledger transfer becomes GL journal lines

There is no REST call here: this is the service's core background feature (BR-GL-30…38). It runs in two stages (ADR-GL-02):

  1. Stage 1, seconds after the ledger posts. Each ledger.changes record (one TB leg) is classified (debit account and credit account looked up in the registry to get a bucket and TB account type), then added into gl_subledger_summary, keyed by exactly the attributes rules can match on (posting date, value date, bucket, side, transfer code, command type, leg index, reason code, counterparty account type). Amounts and leg counts only grow. Kafka offsets are stored in the same DB transaction, so each leg counts exactly once.
  2. Stage 2, at day close. The applicable rule set is applied to every summary row (debit side and credit side separately). Lines are aggregated by GL account, side, product, transfer code, source bucket, trust index, reason code and value date into one LEDGER_DAY journal GLJ-{yyyyMMdd}-L-R{revision} (BR-GL-37).

Each worked example below uses the exact inputs and expected lines of the golden vectors in contracts/provided/data/gl-posting-golden-vectors.v1.json (rule set v1, CoA v0). Amounts are shown in Tk; the wire carries poisha. Each ledger leg becomes exactly one debit and one credit GL line of the same amount.

Do not expect one ledger transfer = one balanced "story" per account. A leg from Rahim's wallet to Karim's wallet maps both sides to 2101. The GL keeps both lines (the day journal aggregates them), so customer-to-customer movements show up as equal debit and credit movement on 2101 and no change in the balance. This is correct: the Operator owes its customers, as a group, the same amount.

5. End-to-end workflows

One sequence diagram per key flow of SPEC §3.4. The numbered steps under each diagram match the arrow numbers. Solid arrows are calls or DB actions; dashed arrows are Kafka messages or replies; grey italic rows are conditions ("alt" branches).

5.1 Ledger ingestion (exactly once)

Rahim's P2P to Karim produces three legs on ledger.changes. The LedgerIngestor reads them in a batch and stores the summary and the new Kafka offset in one DB transaction. If the service crashes before the commit, nothing was stored and the batch is read again; if it crashes after, the stored offset says the batch is done. That is why each leg counts exactly once (ADR-GL-03).

  1. On partition assignment the ingestor reads next_offset of (ledger.changes, p) from gl_ingest_offset (absent ⇒ earliest).
  2. It seeks the Kafka consumer to that offset. Kafka's own committed offset is ignored for correctness.
  3. A batch arrives, e.g. Rahim's three P2P legs to Karim (principal 500 Tk, fee 5 Tk, VAT 0.75 Tk) among others.
  4. Records whose TigerBeetle timestamp is at or below the partition's max_tb_ts are producer re-publications and are skipped (counted duplicate).
  5. Pending legs and voids produce nothing (counted ignored). Value date = localDate(timestamp@Asia/Dhaka − CUTOFF.EOD); posting date = value date unless that period is CLOSED.
  6. Both account ids are looked up in the registry (gl_ledger_account) to get bucket and TB account type, e.g. Rahim → WALLET:C, FEE_INCOME → SYS:FEE_INCOME.
  7. If an account is unknown (its WALLET_UPSERTED has not arrived yet), the ingestor asks the ledger for its accountType.
  8. If the ledger answers with a wallet type, it is stored with source LEDGER_LOOKUP.
  9. Otherwise the leg is deferred (gl_deferred_transfer, cause UNKNOWN_ACCOUNT) and the batch still commits. See workflow 5.5.
  10. One DB transaction: shared advisory locks of the touched days (sorted), one multi-row upsert into gl_subledger_summary (keys sorted, amounts and leg counts added), TB snapshots for trust/system accounts, deferred inserts, dirty flags for late legs, new gl_day rows.
  11. In the same transaction: next_offset = last + 1, max_tb_ts = greatest(…), compare-and-set on the batch start offset.
  12. Exactly one row updated → COMMIT. Summary and offset are now durable together.
  13. Zero rows → another consumer now owns the partition: ROLLBACK and drop the batch (the new owner processes it). Any failure also rolls back and the batch is re-read.
  14. Finally a Kafka offset commit, for lag monitoring only.

5.2 Day close and restatement

At 00:15 Dhaka time on 2026-10-10 (cut-off 00:00 + 15 min grace) the orchestrator tries to close 2026-10-09. Once every partition is past the cut-off, it derives the LEDGER_DAY journal, balances, control totals and checks, all in one transaction (BR-GL-42).

  1. Every minute (ShedLock gl-day-close) the job picks the oldest OPEN day, e.g. 2026-10-09, and checks that now ≥ cut-off (2026-10-09T18:00Z) + 15 min grace.
  2. Every ledger.changes partition must have max_tb_ts ≥ the cut-off…
  3. …or its stored next_offset equals the Kafka end offset (a quiet partition).
  4. No unresolved deferred legs with value date ≤ d, the previous day closed (or d is the GL start date), and the period OPEN (BR-GL-40).
  5. Still not ready 90 min after cut-off → HIGH DAY_CLOSE_BLOCKED exception (auto-resolves when the day closes).
  6. …and a GL_DAY_CLOSE_BLOCKED notification to FIN_MAKER and FIN_CHECKER, repeated every 60 min. Finance may then force-close (UC-20).
  7. Ready: take the exclusive advisory lock of the day (no ingest batch can write into it now), revision += 1, status CLOSING, choose the applicable rule set.
  8. Apply the rule set to every summary row and write GLJ-20261009-L-R1; instantiate template accounts; 1999 lines raise CRITICAL UNMAPPED_POSTING + GL_UNMAPPED_POSTING.
  9. Daily balances (opening = previous closing), control totals per bucket, then checks GL-1…GL-7.
  10. A broken journal, trial balance or summary → ROLLBACK, day FAILED, CRITICAL CLOSE_CHECK_FAILED, GL_DAY_CLOSED_WITH_EXCEPTIONS notification; retried every 5 min.
  11. Write GL_DAY_CLOSED, any GL_EXCEPTION_RAISED (HIGH+) and audit.events to the outbox in the same transaction.
  12. COMMIT with status CLOSED (or CLOSED_WITH_EXCEPTIONS if GL-3/5/6 failed or HIGH+ exceptions are open), dirty = false, certification PENDING.
  13. After the commit: accrual reversals for d+1, the ERP export of (d, revision), and day d+1 is created if missing.

Restatement reuses steps 7–12 for a dirty closed day of an OPEN period: revision + 1, the previous LEDGER_DAY journal becomes SUPERSEDED, GL_DAY_CLOSED is published with restated: true, a new export version replaces the old one, and the certification goes back to PENDING. Later closed days get new balances and control totals (but no new journal) (BR-GL-45).

5.3 Certification by reconciliation (recon.events)

The GL never certifies itself. finance-ops-service (reconciliation) compares the GL's control totals with its own projection of the ledger and announces the outcome on recon.events (BR-GL-70…72).

  1. The GL publishes GL_DAY_CLOSED for 2026-10-09 revision 1.
  2. Reconciliation waits until its own end-of-day for d is closed (same readiness rule, so both books close on identical data).
  3. It calls GET /v1/gl/days/{businessDate}/control-totals?revision=1 (UC-19).
  4. It compares each SYS:* bucket and each trust account exactly, and Σ WALLET:* in aggregate, against its own posted counters.
  5. It publishes RECON_RUN_COMPLETED with type GL_TIEOUT, summary.glTieOut and summary.glRevision.
  6. The GL consumes it (inbox processed_event).
  7. If glRevision ≠ the day's current revision (it was restated meanwhile), ignore.
  8. MATCHED + COMPLETED → certification CERTIFIED, certified_run_id = runId, GL_DAY_CERTIFIED.
  9. Otherwise FAILED: GL_DAY_CERTIFIED with breakCount, CRITICAL CERTIFICATION_FAILED (blocks the month close).
  10. …and a GL_DAY_CERTIFICATION_FAILED notification to FIN_CHECKER.

5.4 Manual journal (maker-checker)

  1. Nusrat sends the 575 Tk bank-charge journal (Dr 5301 / Cr 1201) with Idempotency-Key: mj-20261010-0001.
  2. Validation: balanced, GL-only postable active accounts, open period, date window (BR-GL-60/61).
  3. Store the journal as PENDING_APPROVAL and an approval_request (1 level; 2 if ≥ 1,000,000 Tk).

  9. Outbox: APPROVAL_DECIDED, gl.events GL_JOURNAL_POSTED, audit JOURNAL_POSTED.

5.5 Unknown wallet: deferral, timeout and re-keying

Mitu Store's brand-new agent wallet receives its first leg before its WALLET_UPSERTED reached the GL (Kafka gives no order across topics), and the ledger lookup also fails. The GL never guesses the wallet type (BR-GL-13, BR-GL-34).

  2. Every 30 s the resolver retries the ledger lookup.
  3. Type known → the leg is added to the summary under the posting date computed now (shared day lock); resolution = RESOLVED.

  5. Mitu Store's WALLET_UPSERTED finally arrives; the registry learns WALLET:A.
  6. The resolver re-keys: subtract from the 1999 summary row, add under the correct key (agent wallet → 2102), mark the posting day dirty so it is restated; resolution = REKEYED.

10. FAQ and gotchas for new developers

The ledger shows Rahim's P2P, but the GL report doesn't. Is it broken?

Probably not. The GL is eventually consistent with ledger-service: legs arrive on ledger.changes seconds later (lag p99 < 5 s), and the day's official journal exists only after close (cut-off + 15 min grace, and only when every partition is past the cut-off). Reports on an open day use the overlay and say provisional: true. Check bitspay_gl_ingest_lag_seconds and GET /v1/gl/days/{businessDate} readiness before suspecting a bug.

What happens if the same ledger event is delivered twice?

Two kinds of duplicates exist. (1) Consumer redelivery (crash, rebalance): offsets live in gl_ingest_offset and are updated by compare-and-set on the batch start offset in the same transaction as the summary upsert; 0 rows updated → rollback and drop. (2) Producer re-publication: the ledger may re-publish a window but never reorders, so any record with timestamp ≤ max_tb_ts for its partition is skipped (counted duplicate). Never rely on Kafka commits for correctness, and never add a processed_event row per leg (≈ 1.3 billion rows/day at target load). Other topics do use processed_event.

Can I replay ledger.changes to rebuild the books?

The ledger-derived part is rebuildable from ledger.changes within its 14-day retention. Re-mapping history (a rule fix, a restatement) never needs a Kafka replay, because stage 1 keeps the pre-mapping summary (ADR-GL-02). GL-only journals (manual, opening…) are originals and must be backed up. The GL must be deployed before the first business transfer (OPEN GL-Q8).

A posted journal is wrong. Can I UPDATE it?

No. gl_journal_line rows are never updated or deleted. Corrections are new journals: :reverse for MANUAL / ACCRUAL / AUTO_INTEREST / OPENING_BALANCE, then a new correct journal. LEDGER_DAY journals are never reversed; their day is restated as a new revision and the old one becomes SUPERSEDED. E-money balance corrections are ledger commands owned by other services; the GL books them when they arrive.

A ledger leg arrives for a day in a CLOSED month. Where does it go?

Closed periods never change. The leg's posting date becomes the first day ≥ its value date that is not closed (always in an OPEN period), the line keeps its original valueDate, and a HIGH PRIOR_PERIOD_LATE_EVENT is raised. If the month is still OPEN but the day is closed, the day is marked dirty and restated (INFO LATE_LEDGER_EVENT).

Why is there a 1999 account, and why does a movement on it block a clean close?

1999 is the safety net: a leg is never dropped, so anything no rule matches (or whose wallet type stays unknown for 1 h) lands there with a CRITICAL exception. 1999 is mustBeZero, so GL-5 fails and the day closes CLOSED_WITH_EXCEPTIONS. The fix is data: a new rule set + restatement, or the late wallet fact (re-keying).

Why not mirror each ledger system account 1:1 to one GL account?

Because FEE_INCOME (200) also pays cash-out commission and is swept to the operating bank, and COMMISSION_PAYABLE (220) funds cash-in commission and runs a debit balance. A 1:1 mirror would book commission as negative fee income. Rules split those pools by transfer code and command instead; per-pool control stays exact through source-tagged lines (GL-3b) (ADR-GL-06).

Can I compute VAT or commission in the GL?

Never. The GL only adds amounts the ledger posted (or a maker typed). No multiplication, division, rounding or floating point. Use long poisha and BigDecimal.valueOf(poisha, 2) only for display.

Where do I put a new GL account mapping?

In a new rule set (data, maker-checker, 2 levels), never in Java. Run :validate (986 combinations, 0 errors) and :simulate first. If the ledger adds a command, transfer code or account type, the posting universe must be regenerated (BR-GL-22).

Why can't Finance close the month when everything is closed?

Every day must also be certified by reconciliation (no override, OPEN GL-Q3), with no pending journals and no open CRITICAL exceptions. Read blockingItems in the 409. For isolated local runs, bitspay.gl.certification.required=false makes days NOT_REQUIRED.

Can the GL wait for ledger-service at start-up?

No. Readiness covers Postgres and Kafka only. If the ledger is down, lookups defer legs and day panels show NOT_AVAILABLE; nothing blocks. And a GL outage never affects payments.

Locks: what stops a day closing while a batch is writing into it?

Ingestion takes the shared advisory lock pg_advisory_xact_lock_shared(hashtext('gl-day-<d>')) for every posting date in the batch (sorted); close and restatement take the exclusive lock. Partitions ingest in parallel, and a day is never closed under a running batch.

Personal data?

None, anywhere: not in tables, events, logs or audit. wallet.events carries names and MSISDN, but the GL reads only walletId, walletType and version. transferId/commandId may be logged.
