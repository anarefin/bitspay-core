transaction-service

The payment orchestrator: turns every payment request into exactly one ledger posting or a recorded refusal, and announces the outcome on txn.events.
SVC-02 · Source: docs/SPEC.md (v6) + docs/contracts · For new developers · Example values are illustrative

1. What this service does, in one minute

transaction-service (SVC-02) is the payment orchestrator of Bits Pay and the busiest code path in the platform. "Orchestrator" means it does not hold any money itself: it checks a payment request with other services, then asks ledger-service (the only service that moves money) to post it. Every request ends as exactly one ledger posting or as a recorded refusal, and the result is announced on the Kafka topic txn.events.

Analogy: the bank teller at the counter. Rahim hands the teller a slip: "send 500 Tk to Karim". The teller checks the slip has not been handed in twice (idempotency), looks up both accounts to see they are open (wallet status), asks the compliance desk whether Rahim is within his limits (limits-risk), reads the fee from the price board (pricing), writes the slip into the day book (the intent), and only then asks the vault (ledger) to move the cash. When the vault says "done", the teller stamps the slip COMPLETED and tells everyone (the event). If the vault phone line drops, the teller never guesses: they call the vault back later and ask.

What it owns

  • The payment APIs: quote, P2P (send money), cash-in, cash-out, merchant payment, merchant refund, float transfer, and the internal reversal executor.
  • Query APIs: one transaction by txnRef, and a wallet's 30-day history.
  • Idempotency (no double payments), txnId / txnRef generation, the day-partitioned txn_intent table.
  • An in-memory copy of the fee plans and a fee calculator identical to pricing-service's.
  • Merchant refund maker-checker (request, approve, reject), saved beneficiaries, the recovery sweeper and the exception queue.
  • The txn.events topic (4 event types), written through a transactional outbox.

Example in numbers

Rahim sends 500 Tk to Karim. Plan PLN-P2P-STD v3 charges a 5 Tk fee and 15 % VAT on the fee:

amount         50,000 poisha  (500.00 Tk)
fee               500 poisha  (  5.00 Tk)
VAT 15% of fee     75 poisha  (  0.75 Tk)
total debit    50,575 poisha  (505.75 Tk)  borne by Rahim (PAYER)

1 Tk = 100 poisha. Money is always a whole number of poisha (a Java long), never a decimal.

This service must NEVER (SPEC §1.4)
  1. Talk to TigerBeetle or compute balances. Only ledger-service moves money.
  2. Read or write another service's database. Redis use is limited to: read ws:*, write wsn:*, and its own idem:* keys.
  3. Call wallet-service or pricing-service on the payment path, except for the wallet-status cache-miss fallback and the pricing bootstrap or reload.
  4. Answer COMPLETED before the outcome row and its outbox row are committed.
  5. Mark an INITIATED intent FAILED without asking the ledger (GET /v1/ledger/commands/{commandId}). Never guess.
  6. Move money when wallet status, limits/risk or pricing cannot be read. Refuse with 503 DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE.
  7. Publish to Kafka directly (outbox only), use double/float for money, or log PIN, NID, full MSISDN, tokens, note or merchantReference.
  8. Re-send a ledger command with a body different from the stored intent, or reuse a txnId for a new attempt.
  9. Write a per-payment AUDIT_RECORDED for customer payment outcomes of P2P, CASH_IN, CASH_OUT, MERCHANT_PAYMENT or FLOAT_TRANSFER (their trail is txn.events).
  10. Change anything under contracts/ without a change request.

Personas used on this page: customers Rahim and Karim; agent Mitu Store (the SPEC calls the agent "Salma"); merchant Dhaka Bazar (the SPEC calls it "Nirob Store", category 3101, MICRO grocery); back-office checker Fatema; distributor Padma Distribution. 3. Key concepts and glossary

poisha
The smallest unit of BDT. 100 poisha = 1 Tk. Every amount field ends in Poisha and is an integer (int64).
txnType
The kind of payment. Closed list of 7: P2P, CASH_IN, CASH_OUT, MERCHANT_PAYMENT, MERCHANT_REFUND, FLOAT_TRANSFER, REVERSAL.
txnId
A UUIDv7 (time-ordered UUID) created for each payment attempt. It is also the ledger commandId and the limits-risk reservation id. Its timestamp is the transaction's createdAt.
txnRef
The human-readable reference, 18 chars: prefix + Dhaka date + 00 + 8 random Crockford base32 chars, e.g. PP26100900K3M9QX2A. Prefixes: PP P2P, CI cash-in, CO cash-out, MP merchant payment, MR merchant refund, FT float transfer, RV reversal. Other services must treat it as opaque.
quote
A read-only fee preview (POST /v1/payments/quote) for the confirmation screen. It stores nothing, reserves nothing and needs no idempotency key.
intent
The txn_intent row written with status INITIATED just before the ledger is called (pipeline step 6). It is the service's promise "I am about to post this". The ledger answer later turns it into COMPLETED or FAILED.
Idempotency-Key
A header the client generates once per payment. Sending the same key again with the same body replays the first outcome instead of paying twice. Scope is (X-Actor-Id, key), kept at least 7 days. Same key with a different body gives 409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT.
outcome
The final result of one request: COMPLETED or FAILED (and, only inside a 503, INITIATED = "unknown, retry with the same key"). A replay returns the outcome of that request, not the current status.
PaymentResult
The response body of every payment POST except quote; also embedded as the problem member transaction when the intent was persisted.
problem+json
The standard error body: type, title, status, code, correlationId, plus extension members such as transaction or limitCode.
fee bearer
Who pays the fee: PAYER (sender, added on top), PAYEE (receiver, taken off what they receive, e.g. merchant MDR) or OPERATOR (fee forced to 0). SHARED is reserved and never produced.
MDR
Merchant discount rate: the percentage fee a merchant pays on each payment (1.5 % in the seed plan).
commission / AIT
Money paid to an agent or distributor for a transaction. AIT (advance income tax) is withheld from it, so the agent nets commission − AIT. Paid from FEE_INCOME or COMMISSION_PAYABLE.
wallet status
A one-letter code from the Redis cache: A ACTIVE, P PENDING_KYC, D DORMANT, X DEBIT_BLOCKED, C CREDIT_BLOCKED, F BLOCKED, Q PENDING_CLOSURE, Z CLOSED. It decides whether a wallet may be debited or credited.
wallet type
C customer, A agent, D distributor, M merchant, I institution.
reservation
When limits-risk answers ALLOW it reserves the sender's and receiver's limit counters under txnId. TRANSACTION_COMPLETED commits them, TRANSACTION_FAILED releases them; if no intent was written this service calls the release API.
outbox
A table (svc_transaction.outbox) written in the same DB transaction as the state change. A relay (Debezium, or a polling relay locally) copies rows to Kafka. This guarantees "state changed" and "event sent" never disagree.
re-drive
When a client retries the same key within 20 s and the intent is still INITIATED, the identical ledger command is re-sent (same commandId). The ledger answers EXISTS or OK, so nothing is paid twice.
sweeper
A background job (leader only, every 5 s) that finds intents stuck in INITIATED for over 30 s, asks the ledger what happened, and settles them.
maker-checker
Four-eyes control: one person (the maker) asks, a different person (the checker) approves. Used for enterprise merchant refunds and all staff-made refunds.
reversal
A corrective transaction (RV…) that mirrors an original back from receiver to sender, executed for support-service after its own approval.
product switch
A per-product on/off flag (config plus FEATURE.<PRODUCT> refdata, optionally per channel). When off: 422 SERVICE_DISABLED before anything happens.
X-Actor-* headers
Trusted headers that say who is calling: X-Actor-Type, X-Actor-Id, X-Actor-Wallet-Id, X-Actor-Roles, X-Actor-User-Id, plus X-Channel and X-Device-Id. A wallet holder must send the debited wallet as X-Actor-Wallet-Id.

The 9-step payment pipeline (the order is fixed)

  1. Validate the body and headers, check the product switch and the actor binding. Failure: 400 / 403 / 422 SERVICE_DISABLED, nothing stored.
  2. Idempotency claim on Redis critical (idem:{actorId}:{key}). Redis down: 503. A hit: replay, re-drive or 409.
  3. Wallet status for both sides (one pipelined Redis read, wallet-service fallback). Unknown: 404; wrong type: 400; blocked direction: 422 persisted.
  4. Risk evaluate at limits-risk (reserves limits). Non-ALLOW: 422 persisted FAILED; timeout: 503 plus reservation release.
  5. Pricing in memory, using the sender's monthly count returned by risk.
  6. Insert the intent INITIATED (with a 7-day idempotency look-back).
  7. Ledger command with commandId = txnId.
  8. One DB transaction: conditional UPDATE to the final state plus the outbox row(s).
  9. Reply 200 or the mapped problem with transaction.

REVERSAL skips steps 3 to 5 (no wallet-status gate, no risk, no pricing).

4.1 Quote

Read-only fee preview used on every confirmation screen (PAY-011).

UC-01Show the fee before the customer confirmsquote
POST/v1/payments/quote

Karim wants to cash out 500 Tk at Mitu Store. Before he enters his PIN, the app asks for a quote. Plan PLN-CASHOUT-STD v2: fee = 1.5 % of 50,000 = 750 poisha (7.50 Tk); VAT = 15 % of 750 = 112.5, rounded half-even to 112; total debit = 50,000 + 750 + 112 = 50,862 poisha (508.62 Tk). The screen shows "You pay 508.62 Tk". Nothing is stored and no limit is reserved.

Triggered by
CUSTOMER (P2P, CASH_OUT, MERCHANT_PAYMENT), AGENT (CASH_IN, FLOAT_TRANSFER), DISTRIBUTOR (FLOAT_TRANSFER), STAFF OPS_CHECKER/FIN_CHECKER (FLOAT_TRANSFER); X-Actor-Wallet-Id = fromWalletId. No Idempotency-Key.
Rules
BR-TX-16..19. txnType must be one of the 5 quotable types (MERCHANT_REFUND, REVERSAL → 400). Same actor, type, direction and float pair/hierarchy checks as the real payment. Reads the sender's monthly count from GET /v1/limits/wallets/{walletId}/usage (300 ms, no retry) to apply any free allowance; if it cannot be read the fee is computed without the allowance and feeIsUpperBound: true (the real fee can only be lower). The usage call is skipped when the plan has freeMonthlyCount = 0. feeBearer is never null (no plan → PAYER); creditToPayeePoisha = amount − (PAYEE ? fee + vat : 0). The payment re-evaluates the fee at execution.
Errors
400 VALIDATION_FAILED, 403 ACTOR_NOT_ALLOWED, 404 NOT_FOUND, 422 WALLET_NOT_TRANSACTABLE, 422 SERVICE_DISABLED, 503 DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE. A quote error never carries transaction; an unreadable usage count is never an error.
Emits
Nothing.

5. End-to-end workflows

One sequence diagram per key flow of SPEC §3.4. Read top to bottom; solid arrows are calls, dashed arrows are replies, a red X is a call that never got an answer. The numbered list under each diagram matches the arrow numbers.

F1. Send money happy path (Rahim → Karim, 500 Tk)

The reference flow. Every other payment type follows the same shape.

  1. Rahim's app sends the P2P request with a fresh Idempotency-Key.
  2. Body, headers and the product switch are checked; Rahim's X-Actor-Wallet-Id must equal fromWalletId.
  3. The idempotency claim is a single Lua script on Redis critical (step 2).
  4. NEW: nobody used this key before, so continue.
  5. Both wallets are read in one round trip from the wallet-status cache (step 3).
  6. Both are Active customers (C): Rahim may be debited, Karim may be credited.
  7. limits-risk is asked whether Rahim may send and Karim may receive (step 4).
  8. ALLOW: limits are reserved under txnId; Karim's maximum balance and Rahim's monthly P2P count come back.
  9. The fee is computed in memory from the snapshot (step 5): 500 + 75 poisha.
  10. The intent row is written INITIATED (step 6). From here on the request is "persisted".
  11. The ledger is asked to post the linked chain (step 7): principal, fee leg, VAT leg, with Karim's balance cap.
  12. The ledger posted it (OK, or EXISTS on a re-send).
  13. One DB transaction flips the row to COMPLETED and writes the outbox row (step 8). Only now is it safe to say "done".
  14. Reply 200 (step 9).

F2. Cash-in with agent commission (Mitu Store → Rahim, 1,000 Tk)

Shows how commission is posted from COMMISSION_PAYABLE in the same ledger chain.

  1. The agent app sends the cash-in with its own wallet as agentWalletId.
  2. Idempotency and wallet status as in F1; wallet types must be agent → customer.
  3. Risk evaluation for CASH_IN.
  4. Allowed, possibly with Rahim's maximum balance.
  5. Pricing: no fee, but 0.40 % commission (400) with 10 % AIT (40) for the payer, i.e. the agent.
  6. Intent written.
  7. The ledger command carries the commission fields.
  8. Posted. The chain paid Mitu Store 360 net and 40 to the tax account in the same all-or-nothing posting (see the note).
  9. Outcome and TRANSACTION_COMPLETED committed together.
  10. Reply 200.

F3. Cash-out with a ledger refusal (Karim → Mitu Store, 500 Tk)

Shows the failure branch: the ledger refuses for lack of funds and the refusal is persisted and announced.

  1. Karim requests the cash-out over USSD.
  2. Usual checks; the agent wallet must really be type A.
  3. Risk evaluation for CASH_OUT.
  4. Allowed; his monthly cash-out count is 2 (used for a free allowance if the plan had one).
  5. Pricing per golden vector G2.
  6. Intent written.
  7. Ledger command sent.
  8. Branch "refused": the ledger says Karim cannot cover 508.62 Tk.
  9. The row becomes FAILED and TRANSACTION_FAILED is written (this also releases his limit reservation at limits-risk).
  10. Reply 422 with the FAILED transaction in the problem body.
  11. Branch "posted": the ledger answers OK.
  12. Row COMPLETED + outbox.
  13. Reply 200.

F4. Merchant QR payment with MDR borne by the merchant (Rahim → Dhaka Bazar, 350 Tk)

Shows feeBearer PAYEE: the customer pays exactly the price.

  1. Rahim confirms the QR payment.
  2. Checks; the merchant's category (3101, MICRO grocery) is taken from the cache, not the request.
  3. Risk evaluation.
  4. Allowed.
  5. The plan matching the resolved category 3101 wins; fee and VAT are taken from the merchant's side.
  6. Intent written.
  7. Ledger command with the PAYEE bearer, so fee legs debit the merchant.
  8. Posted.
  9. Outcome + outbox; Dhaka Bazar's app is notified from the event.
  10. Reply 200.

F5. Merchant refund (partial), direct or with maker-checker

Two entry routes, one execution path. The original payment row is locked so two refunds can never exceed it.

  1. (maker-checker route) The maker creates a refund request.
  2. Validated without moving money; approval PENDING; approval event and audit written.
  3. A different person (the checker) approves the final level.
  4. The approve call runs the refund itself, as the maker, with the deterministic key apr-{approvalId}.
  5. (direct route) A non-enterprise merchant calls the refund endpoint directly.
  6. Cheap first check of the original without a lock.
  7. Idempotency claim and wallet status for merchant → customer.
  8. Risk evaluation for MERCHANT_REFUND.
  9. Lock the original row, re-check the remainder, book the amount as pending and write the refund intent, all in one transaction.
  10. Ledger posts merchant → customer.
  11. Posted.
  12. One transaction finalises the refund, updates the original's refunded total and status, and writes both events plus the audit row.
  13. Reply 200.

F6. Float transfer (Padma Distribution → Mitu Store top-up)

The hierarchy check uses a local copy of partner relationships fed by partner.events.

  1. The distributor (or treasury-service forwarding its headers) asks for the top-up.
  2. Checks, including the allowed pair list.
  3. Is Mitu Store a direct child of Padma, and are both ACTIVE?
  4. Yes. (A STAFF caller skips this check; the override is audited.)
  5. Risk evaluation.
  6. Allowed.
  7. No fee; intent written.
  8. Ledger command.
  9. Posted.
  10. Outcome + outbox.
  11. Reply 200.

F7. Internal reversal (support-service after its maker-checker)

No wallet-status gate, no risk call, no pricing and no max-balance constraint: this is a corrective credit.

  1. support-service calls with the approved case.
  2. The original is locked and its reversibility checked.
  3. The original is flagged and the REVERSAL intent written in the same transaction.
  4. The ledger mirrors the original, voiding the dispute hold first. Steps 3 to 5 of the pipeline were skipped (see the note).
  5. Posted.
  6. Both rows and both events committed together (plus audit).
  7. Reply 200 with the RV… result.

F8. Crash after the ledger post, before the outcome commit → sweeper

Why the sweeper exists: money moved but the pod died before recording it.

  1. Pod A writes the intent.
  2. The ledger posts the money.
  3. Pod A dies before step 8. The row is stuck INITIATED.
  4. The sweeper leader scans for intents older than 30 s every 5 s.
  5. It asks the ledger what happened to that commandId.
  6. The ledger has it.
  7. Conditional update to COMPLETED plus the event, exactly once.
  8. (alternative) The ledger has no record: mark the first check.
  9. Ask again at least 60 s later.
  10. Still nothing: FAILED LEDGER_NOT_POSTED and TRANSACTION_FAILED.

F9. Ledger timeout, same-key retry, re-drive, conflict

What the client must do when the answer is "unknown".

  1. First attempt (steps 1–6 not shown).
  2. Ledger command sent.
  3. No answer within 500 ms: the money may or may not have moved.
  4. The service never guesses: 503 with the INITIATED transaction, retryable: true.
  5. The client retries with the same key and body.
  6. Redis already holds the claim.
  7. Same request hash, so this is a genuine retry.
  8. The intent is still INITIATED and only 3 s old.
  9. Re-drive: the identical command is re-sent; the ledger deduplicates by commandId.
  10. Outcome recorded once.
  11. Same txnRef as the first attempt.
  12. A different body under the same key is a client bug.
  13. Rejected with 409.

F10. Quote for the cash-out confirmation screen

Read-only; the usage call only affects the free monthly allowance.

  1. Karim's app asks for a quote; no key is needed because nothing is stored.
  2. Same switch and actor rules as the payment.
  3. Wallet status read and validated.
  4. Optional usage read for the free allowance.
  5. (usage readable) Karim has 2 cash-outs this month.
  6. Fee evaluated with the real count.
  7. (usage unavailable) Evaluate as if the allowance were used up and flag the fee as an upper bound.
  8. Reply with the quote.

10. FAQ and gotchas for new developers

Why must the client send an Idempotency-Key, and when may it reuse one?

Mobile networks drop responses. Without a key, a retry could pay twice. Reuse the same key (with the same body) only after a 503, a timeout, a 5xx or a connection failure. Any definitive 400/403/404/409/422 is final for that key: a new attempt needs a new key and gets a new txnId/txnRef. Never reuse a key after 72 h; for anything older than 24 h first check GET /v1/transactions?walletId={from}&clientReference={key}.

What does a replay return: the original result or the current status?

The outcome of that request. If Rahim's P2P completed and was later reversed, replaying the key still returns COMPLETED. Use GET /v1/transactions/{txnRef} for the current state (REVERSED).

The ledger timed out. Did the money move?

Unknown, and the service never guesses. It keeps the intent INITIATED and answers 503 DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE with transaction.status: INITIATED. Retrying the same key within 20 s re-drives the identical ledger command (same commandId, so the ledger deduplicates). Later, the sweeper asks the ledger and settles it within about 30 to 95 s.

Why can't I just mark a stuck intent FAILED after a timeout?

Because the ledger may have posted it. Marking FAILED would release limits and tell the customer the money did not move while it did. The rule is: FAILED only after GET /v1/ledger/commands/{commandId} says REJECTED, or says "not posted" twice at least 60 s apart. Even then the late-post verifier re-checks 10 to 15 min later.

Does this service retry calls to other services?

No automatic retries on the payment path (ledger, risk, wallet status): retries are explicit (client same-key retry, re-drive, sweeper). Exceptions: the ledger lookup (2 retries), the reservation release (3 retries with backoff) and the pricing bootstrap (5 retries).

When is the limit reservation released?

A persisted FAILED row needs nothing: TRANSACTION_FAILED releases it. If risk may have reserved but no intent was committed (503 at steps 4–6, intent deadline exceeded, idempotency takeover, refund re-check failure) the service calls POST /v1/limits/reservations/{txnId}:release asynchronously. Not after a risk 409. limits-risk's 10-minute expiry is the backstop.

How is a reversal different from a refund?

A refund is a merchant giving money back for a merchant payment (full or partial, many allowed, within 30 days, fee 0, MDR not returned). A reversal is a support correction that mirrors any completed non-refund transaction back, at most once, optionally with fee and commission. A refunded or partially refunded payment cannot be reversed (409); a MERCHANT_REFUND row cannot be reversed either.

Why does a reversal skip wallet status, risk and pricing?

It is a corrective credit approved by support, so limits and fees must not block it and no max-balance cap applies. The ledger still rejects closed accounts (422 WALLET_NOT_TRANSACTABLE) and insufficient funds on the original receiver.

Why two events for one reversal, and why might they arrive out of order?

TRANSACTION_COMPLETED describes the RV row (key = original receiver) and TRANSACTION_REVERSED describes the original (key = original sender). Kafka orders only within a key, so consumers must correlate by data.txnRef/txnId.

Why is money a long of poisha, and how is VAT rounded?

Floating point cannot represent 0.10 exactly. All maths uses long and BigDecimal with HALF_EVEN (banker's rounding): 112.5 → 112, 78.75 → 79, 17.5 → 18. The evaluator must match pricing-service byte for byte; the shared golden vectors prove it. Never "improve" it locally.

Two pods processed the same intent (request, re-drive and sweeper). Is that a problem?

No. Every final write is UPDATE … WHERE status = 'INITIATED'. The loser updates 0 rows, re-reads the stored state, and writes no event. Ledger ids are deterministic from commandId, so a double post is impossible.

Why does a non-party get 404 instead of 403 on GET /v1/transactions/{txnRef}?

So that guessing references does not reveal which ones exist.
