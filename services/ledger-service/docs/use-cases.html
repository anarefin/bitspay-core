ledger-service

The only door to TigerBeetle: every taka that moves in Bits Pay moves through one of its 17 double-entry commands, and no other service holds a balance.
SVC-01 · Source: docs/SPEC.md (v6) + docs/contracts (contractVersion 1.0.0) · For new developers · Example values are illustrative

1. What this service does, in one minute

Analogy. Think of a bank's master ledger book, kept by one very strict clerk. Nobody else may write in the book. Every entry takes money out of one line and puts the same amount into another line, so the book always balances. Entries are written in ink: a mistake is never rubbed out, it is fixed by writing a new, opposite entry. If you hand the clerk the same slip twice, he sees the slip number and does not write it twice. ledger-service is that clerk; TigerBeetle (TB, a database built only for double-entry money transfers) is the book.

Other services decide whether money should move: transaction-service checks limits and fees, wallet-service knows if a wallet is active, pricing-service works out fees and commissions. They then send ledger-service a command such as post-p2p. The ledger turns the command into a set of TB transfers ("legs"), submits them so that they all apply or none do, and answers OK, EXISTS (already done) or REJECTED (and why).

What it owns

  • All TB accounts: customer, agent, distributor, merchant and institution wallets, the trust (bank mirror) accounts and the fixed system accounts (fees, VAT, commission, AIT, campaign funding, suspense, adjustment clearing, hold control).
  • The 17 business commands and their leg templates.
  • Holds (liens), reversals, account close, disbursement batches.
  • Balances, the system balance sheet, command lookup and transfer history.
  • The ledger.changes Kafka stream (one event per business transfer).

What it does NOT do

  • Business checks needing other services' data (wallet status, limits, fees, same-person rules). Callers do those first.
  • Maker-checker approvals: callers approve before calling.
  • Statements, end-of-day snapshots, general ledger: built by others from ledger.changes.
  • Interop settlement, multi-currency (only BDT, ledger = 1), posting a hold.
  • Calling any other Bits Pay service. It calls nobody and consumes no Kafka topic.
ledger-service must NEVER (SPEC §1.4):
  1. Let any other service reach TigerBeetle, or expose raw TB transfer creation over HTTP.
  2. Modify or delete a posted transfer. Corrections are new compensating commands.
  3. Generate a random transfer id. Every transfer id is transferId(commandId, legIndex).
  4. Void a hold, post, or reverse on a timeout or unknown TB state. Unknown outcome → 503; the caller retries with the same commandId.
  5. Call another Bits Pay service synchronously, consume another service's topic, or read another service's schema.
  6. Use double/float anywhere. TB 128-bit values are UInt128/BigInteger, narrowed to long only with an overflow check.
  7. Publish technical transfers (codes 9001–9099) on ledger.changes, in transferIds, in GET …/transfers or in the command lookup.
  8. Accept the technical accounts 900/910 as a parameter, or report them in any balance API.
  9. Change leg ids, transferIds or ledger.changes because of max-balance enforcement.
  10. Turn a REJECTED into anything else on replay: a REJECTED is final for its commandId.
  11. Put PII anywhere: the ledger holds ids and amounts only.

Money is always an integer number of poisha (1 Tk = 100 poisha), sent as int64. In this page "500.00 Tk (50,000)" means 50,000 poisha.

2. Where it sits

Callers on the left send REST commands and reads. The ledger writes money to TigerBeetle and metadata to its own Postgres schema svc_ledger. A second run role of the same image, the CDC bridge (CDC = change data capture: reading every committed change as a stream), receives TB's own change feed through RabbitMQ and republishes business transfers to Kafka ledger.changes. Audit rows go to admin-service's audit.events topic through an outbox (a table written in the same DB transaction and relayed to Kafka later).

3. Key concepts & glossary

Double-entry bookkeeping in five minutes

Every movement of money is written twice: once as a debit on the account the money leaves, and once as a credit on the account it enters, for the same amount. Because each transfer adds the same number to "total debits" and "total credits", the sum over all accounts of (credits − debits) is always zero. If it is not, something is broken. That single check is what makes a ledger trustworthy.

"Debit" and "credit" are not "bad" and "good". They are just the two columns. What a column means depends on the account's normal side:

  • Credit-normal accounts (all wallets, FEE_INCOME, VAT_PAYABLE…): balance = credits − debits. Money coming in is a credit. These are what Bits Pay owes people (e-money is a liability).
  • Debit-normal accounts (TRUST_SETTLEMENT): balance = debits − credits. A trust account mirrors real money sitting in a partner bank. Issuing e-money debits it (mirror goes up) and credits a wallet.

TB transferOne immutable movement: id, debit_account_id, credit_account_id, amount, code (what kind, e.g. 1001 P2P_PRINCIPAL), flags, and three user-data fields. Here user_data_128 = commandId, user_data_64 = command type, user_data_32 = leg index.
CommandOne business request to the ledger, e.g. post-p2p, identified by a caller-chosen commandId (UUID). There are 17 command types (codes.yaml commandType).
LegOne TB transfer inside a command. Positions are fixed: for payments, leg 0 principal, 1 fee, 2 VAT, 3 commission, 4 AIT, 5–8 technical "bounds" legs. A skipped leg (amount 0) leaves a gap; later legs never shift.
Linked chainAll legs of one command, submitted with the TB linked flag on every leg except the last. TB applies the whole chain or none of it (atomic). A chain is never split across TB requests.
Pending / posted / voidedA normal transfer is posted at once. A pending transfer (flag pending) reserves money in the *_pending counters without moving it, which is how a hold (lien) works. Later it is either posted (flag post_pending_transfer, not exposed here) or voided (flag void_pending_transfer), which releases the reservation.
Ledger balance / held / availableFor credit-normal accounts: ledger balance = credits_posted − debits_posted; held = debits_pending; available = ledger − held. For TRUST: ledger = debits_posted − credits_posted, held = credits_pending (BR-01-30).
ledger fieldTB's currency partition. Always 1 (BDT). 2 (USD) is reserved and unused.
code fieldOn an account: the account type (100…910). On a transfer: the transfer code (1001–1016, 2001–2004 business; 9001–9005 technical).
Technical leg / accountNet-zero plumbing the outside world never sees: bounds legs 9001–9004, the close transfer 9005, and accounts 900 BOUNDS_CONTROL and 910 BOUNDS_OPERATOR.
Max balance (balance bounds)A caller may send constraints.destinationMaxBalancePoisha. The ledger appends four technical legs that make TB itself fail the chain if the receiver would end above the limit (§7.4).
Transfer id derivationtransferId(commandId, legIndex) = first 16 bytes of SHA-256(commandId bytes ‖ leg byte), shown as a UUID. The same command always produces the same ids, which is what makes retries safe.
IdempotencySending the same request twice has the same effect as once. Keyed on commandId only; the Idempotency-Key header is optional and ignored (just logged).
JournalPostgres table command_journal: one row per command result with the request hash, so replays can be answered and changed bodies detected. Plus command_rejection for rejections decided before TB.
BatcherTbBatcher collects chains from many concurrent HTTP requests and sends them to TB as one request (lanes online and bulk).
Trust account / e-moneyBangladesh Bank rules require every taka of e-money to be backed by real money in a trust bank account. Each such bank account has a TB mirror account 00000000-0000-0000-0000-0000000300nn. Total e-money must equal the total trust mirror.
Fee bearerWho pays fee + VAT: PAYER (sender), PAYEE (receiver, e.g. merchant MDR) or OPERATOR (waived, fee = VAT = 0). SHARED is reserved and gives 400.
Commission / AITCommission is paid to agents/distributors. AIT (advance income tax) is withheld from it and credited to AIT_PAYABLE. The beneficiary gets commission − AIT.
MDRMerchant discount rate: the fee a merchant pays on a payment (fee bearer PAYEE).
CDCChange data capture: TB's built-in AMQP feed (tigerbeetle amqp) of every committed transfer, consumed by the bridge.

4. Features & use cases

The contract (openapi.yaml) has 29 operations: 17 commands (one literal path each), 2 account-lifecycle operations (create, close), 6 reads (including the system balance sheet) and 4 internal operations. Every one has a card below. Command cards include a worked leg table: which account is debited and credited, by how much, with which transfer code, and the balances before and after.

Rules common to every command (SPEC §5.1.1, §5.1.4, BR-01-15)
  • Headers: X-Actor-Type: SERVICE (SYSTEM only in the local profile), X-Actor-Id = the caller's bare service name, X-Channel, X-Correlation-Id. Idempotency-Key is optional and ignored.
  • Validation order: headers → caller policy (403) → structure (400) → stored pre-TB rejection → caller-specific rules → money rules (REJECTED INVALID_AMOUNT) → account existence and type guard → TigerBeetle.
  • 400 vs REJECTED: 400 VALIDATION_FAILED = broken request, nothing stored, never retry unchanged. HTTP 200 REJECTED = a business "no", journaled and final for that commandId.
  • Amounts are poisha, 1..1013; txnRef is an opaque 1..32 [A-Z0-9-] string (all commands except 8, 14, 15).
  • HTTP errors on every command: 400 VALIDATION_FAILED, 403 ACTOR_NOT_ALLOWED, 409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT (same commandId, different body), 503 DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE (outcome unknown: retry with the same commandId or use the lookup).
  • Every committed business leg appears on Kafka ledger.changes as one LEDGER_TRANSFER_POSTED event (published by the CDC bridge, not by the request thread).
How TB results become rejection codes (SPEC §7.6). exceeds_credits → INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS (detail "leg n: exceeds_credits"); exceeds_debits on leg 6 → MAX_BALANCE_EXCEEDED; exceeds_debits on a credited TRUST account → INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS + TRUST_ATTRIBUTION_EXCEEDED; other exceeds_debits → OTHER; debit_account_not_found / credit_account_not_found → ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND; debit_account_already_closed / credit_account_already_closed → ACCOUNT_CLOSED; pending_transfer_not_found / _already_voided / _already_posted / _expired on a void leg → HOLD_NOT_FOUND (same-command void → EXISTS); accounts_must_be_different, amount_must_not_be_zero, overflows_* → INVALID_AMOUNT; accounts_must_have_the_same_ledger, transfer_must_have_the_same_ledger_as_accounts → OTHER + alert; exists_with_different_* → HTTP 409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT; id_already_failed → the journaled original rejection, else OTHER (id_already_failed); anything else → OTHER with the raw TB name in detail. Full table in §8.

5. End-to-end workflows

The six key flows of SPEC §3.4. Arrow numbers match the numbered steps under each diagram.

F1: P2P with max balance (Rahim → Karim, 500 Tk)

  1. transaction-service sends post-p2p for Rahim → Karim 500 Tk (fee 5, VAT 0.75, Karim max 300,000 Tk). Its commandId is its own txnId.
  2. The ledger checks headers (X-Actor-Type: SERVICE), the caller policy (403 ACTOR_NOT_ALLOWED) and the JSON shape (400 VALIDATION_FAILED).
  3. It asks Postgres whether this commandId was already rejected before reaching TB (a stored rejection would be replayed).
  4. No stored rejection: carry on.
  5. Account-type guard: P2P must go from a 100 (CUSTOMER_WALLET) to a 100. Money rules: from ≠ to, OPERATOR bearer ⇒ fee = VAT = 0, and so on.
  6. LegTemplates builds legs 0 (principal), 1 (fee), 2 (VAT) and the four technical bounds legs 5–8. Each id is transferId(commandId, legIndex), never random.
  7. The 7-transfer linked chain goes into the online lane of the batcher.
  8. The batcher sends one TB request that also holds other callers' chains (up to 8,189 transfers or 500 µs).
  9. TB answers with failures only; an empty list means everything was applied.
  10. The batcher hands each request its own slice of the results.
  11. ResultResolver (BR-01-14) turns per-leg results into one outcome: all ok → OK.
  12. HTTP 200 with result: OK, the three business transferIds (technical legs never listed) and postedAt.
  13. The journal row (request hash, result, ids) is written after responding (write-behind, ADR-02). TB is the authority, so the response never waits for Postgres.

F2: Replay / crash recovery (same commandId again)

  1. A caller retries (timeout, sweeper, crash recovery) with the same commandId.
  2. First check: was this command rejected before TB?
  3. Yes: compare the SHA-256 of the canonical body (without metadata) with the stored hash.
  4. Same body → the stored REJECTED again (final). Different body → 409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT. TB is never called.
  5. No: resubmit the chain. Because ids are derived, TB recognises them.
  6. TB answers per leg: exists, linked_event_failed, id_already_failed (earlier failure) or exists_with_different_* (body changed).
  7. The ledger reads the journal row for this commandId (waits up to 50 ms if the write-behind row is not there yet).
  8. Body changed → 409. Never retry; it is a caller bug.
  9. Originally rejected by TB → the same REJECTED and rejectionCode (or OTHER / id_already_failed if the journal row has expired).
  10. Originally posted → look up the legs to get the original posting time.
  11. Answer EXISTS with the original ids and postedAt. Money moved exactly once.

F3: Unknown outcome (TB timeout) and resolution by lookup

  1. transaction-service posts Karim's cash-out at Mitu Store.
  2. The ledger submits the chain to TB.
  3. TB does not answer within tb.request-timeout-ms (300 ms online). The money may or may not have moved.
  4. The ledger never guesses: it answers 503 DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE with retryable: true.
  5. The caller resolves the outcome with the command lookup (it could also just retry the same POST).
  6. The ledger derives the possible leg ids 0..8 and looks them up in TB (plus command_rejection / journal).
  7. found: true, result: OK → posted. found: false, result: null → not posted.
  8. Not posted → the caller re-posts with the same commandId and body. If it had in fact been posted, the answer would be EXISTS, so this is always safe.

F4: Wrong-transfer reversal with hold release

  1. After support approves, transaction-service asks to reverse Rahim's P2P and release the hold on Karim.
  2. Claim first (ADR-05): one claim row per original command, inserted before TB is touched.
  3. Someone else already reversed (or is reversing) it: journal the rejection synchronously…
  4. …and answer REJECTED ALREADY_REVERSED.
  5. Look up the original's business legs 0..4 (missing leg 0 → ORIGINAL_NOT_FOUND).
  6. Check the hold: OPEN, on the original leg-0 credit account (Karim), created by support-service with DISPUTE_HOLD or WRONG_TRANSFER_HOLD. Otherwise HOLD_NOT_FOUND.
  7. Build the chain: leg 0 voids the hold, legs 1..n mirror the original legs (debit and credit swapped, code 1013).
  8. Submit the chain. All legs apply or none do.
  9. TB applies it.
  10. One Postgres transaction: hold → RELEASED (released_via = REVERSAL), claim → POSTED, journal row, outbox row for audit LEDGER_REVERSAL_POSTED.
  11. Answer OK with the transfer ids.

F5: CDC bridge (AMQP mode)

  1. TigerBeetle streams every committed transfer in timestamp order to the platform container tb-cdc.
  2. tb-cdc publishes one JSON message per transfer (with both accounts' balance snapshots) to exchange tb.cdc; queue ledger.cdc receives it.
  3. RabbitMQ delivers to exactly one active bridge instance (prefetch 2,000).
  4. The bridge drops technical transfers (codes 9001–9099) and maps the rest to LEDGER_TRANSFER_POSTED.
  5. For command types 10 and 11 only, it reads reasonCode from the committed journal (waits up to 2 s, else null).
  6. Send the batch to Kafka with the idempotent producer (acks=all), key = debitAccountId.
  7. Kafka acknowledges every record.
  8. One transaction: add deltas to account_type_total only for transfers newer than the cursor, then move the cursor (so a redelivery is applied once).
  9. Finally ack RabbitMQ. A crash before this means redelivery: duplicate Kafka events (consumers dedupe on eventId), but the projection is never double-counted.

Poll fallback (cdc.mode=poll): every 50 ms the bridge calls query_transfers from the cursor, with leadership by Postgres advisory lock hashtext('ledger-cdc'). Same publish/commit loop, same cursor, so switching modes is safe (it may re-publish a window).

F6: Account close (wallet closure)

  1. wallet-service moves the residual balance to another wallet of the same person (command 17).
  2. Payout posted; the source wallet is now at zero.
  3. wallet-service asks to close the source account (no body).
  4. The ledger reads the account's counters from TB.
  5. Not empty → 409 STATE_CONFLICT with reason BALANCE_NOT_ZERO or OPEN_HOLDS (a system or trust account gives SYSTEM_ACCOUNT).
  6. Empty → one zero-amount technical transfer, code 9005, flags pending | closing_debit, id from the derived closeCommandId. TB marks the account closed; SUSPENSE stays open.
  7. ok (or exists if already closed).
  8. Record closed_at and close_command_id plus the audit LEDGER_ACCOUNT_CLOSED in one transaction.
  9. Answer OK (or EXISTS = already closed).

10. FAQ & gotchas for new devs

Why is commandId so important? What about Idempotency-Key?

The ledger's idempotency is keyed on commandId only. Same commandId + same body → same transfer ids → TB says exists → you get EXISTS with the original transferIds and postedAt. Same commandId + different body → 409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT. The comparison hashes the canonical JSON (sorted keys, no whitespace) without metadata, so changing metadata.channel is not a conflict. Idempotency-Key is optional and ignored (a missing one is logged and counted by bitspay_ledger_missing_idempotency_key_total).

How are transfer ids derived, and why not random?

transferId(commandId, legIndex) = first 16 bytes of SHA-256(16 UUID bytes ‖ 1 leg byte), rendered as a lower-case UUID; if the result is 0 or 2128−1, add 128 to the leg byte and recompute. A retry, a sweeper or a crashed pod re-sending the command produces exactly the same ids, so TB itself refuses to apply them twice. Golden vector: 0192f3c1-7a2b-7c00-9a51-3f2c8e1d0a11, leg 0 → fc214b7c-f80c-8143-4f0e-107e2518a302. Any service may recompute ids to match transferIds or ledger.changes; only the ledger creates transfers. Never pass UUID bytes straight to the TB Java client: use UInt128.asBytes(lo, hi).

I got a 503 / timeout. Did the money move?

Maybe. A 503 means "unknown", never "failed". Do not pick a new commandId and do not compensate. Either retry with the same commandId and body (you will get OK or EXISTS), or call GET /v1/ledger/commands/{commandId} and apply the interpretation rule (F3). The ledger itself never voids, posts or reverses on a timeout.

Why is a REJECTED final, even if the account is fixed later?

So a retry storm can never turn a "no" into a "yes" for the same business intent. TB-level rejections are remembered by TB (id_already_failed); rejections decided before TB (guards, money rules, ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND from the cache) are stored in command_rejection before responding. To try again after a REJECTED, use a new commandId derived from attempt n+1. Never retry a 400 or 403 unchanged, and never retry a 409.

What does batching mean for me?

TbBatcher packs chains from many concurrent HTTP requests into one TB request (up to 8,189 transfers or 500 µs). Your chain is never split. The online lane carries commands 1–7, 9–11, 14–17; the bulk lane (8, 12, 13) has one request in flight and yields to online, so a million-row salary run does not slow P2P. If TB times out, every request in that batch gets 503. A disbursement call's rows stay in one TB request in row order, which is why a payer running dry rejects only the later rows.

Why can a wallet balance never go negative?

Not because of an if in Java: TB enforces it. Wallets (100–140) and CAMPAIGN_FUNDING carry debits_must_not_exceed_credits, which also counts pending debits (holds). Trust accounts carry the mirror flag credits_must_not_exceed_debits. There is no race between "check" and "post" because TB checks while posting. Note that COMMISSION_PAYABLE, SUSPENSE and ADJUSTMENT_CLEARING can be negative by design.

Reversal vs void vs refund: which is which?

Void cancels a pending transfer (a hold): the reserved money was never moved (UC-15, or leg 0 of a reversal). Reversal (UC-07) creates new mirror transfers (code 1013) that move posted money back; the original is never modified. Only one reversal per original, ever (the claim), and a reversal cannot be reversed. Refund (UC-05) is just another forward payment from merchant to customer. Posting a hold (two-phase external hold) is not exposed.

Why do the max-balance legs not show up anywhere?

Legs 5–8 (codes 9001–9004) are technical and net zero. They exist only to make TB fail the chain if the receiver would exceed the limit. They are never in transferIds, the lookup, the history or ledger.changes. Identify technical transfers by code (≥ 9000), never by legIndex: a reversal's leg 5 is a business leg. The limit value comes from the caller per command; the ledger stores no limits.

Why are some journal rows written after the response?

For OK results of commands 1–6 and 12, TB's answer is authoritative and fast; waiting for Postgres would add latency to every payment (ADR-02). The journal row follows within about 5 ms (group commit). If it is lost, TB's exists_with_different_* still catches conflicting replays. Everything else (rejections, back-office commands, holds, reversals) is written synchronously, together with registry and audit rows.

walletsTotalPoisha vs eMoneyLiabilityPoisha?

A naming trap. walletsTotalPoisha is wallets only (trust total minus system balances). eMoneyLiabilityPoisha is wallets plus e-money system accounts, and always equals trustMirrorTotalPoisha. byWalletType is eventually consistent (CDC projection), so check projectionLagMs.

Other gotchas

  • Never use double/float. Poisha are long; TB counters are u128 (BigInteger), narrowed with an overflow check. BOUNDS_CONTROL counters grow by L on every bounded chain: always u128.
  • Only client.TbGateway imports com.tigerbeetle.* (ArchUnit test).
  • Every non-technical account has the TB history flag, and flags are immutable: on a laptop with an old TB data file, run make reset.
  • Skipped legs keep their index: a cash-in with no fee has legs 0, 3, 4.
  • Accounts 900/910 are never accepted as a parameter (→ ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND / 404) and never reported.
  • release-hold → REJECTED HOLD_NOT_FOUND means "already released" for the caller.
  • Callers may branch on detail only for the listed tokens (NOT_REVERSIBLE, ACCOUNT_TYPE_MISMATCH, TRUST_ATTRIBUTION_EXCEEDED, id_already_failed, IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT); anything else is free text.
  • No PII: log ids and amounts only.
